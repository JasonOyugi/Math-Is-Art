# CrossBoundary: reference design study

Captured 7 November 2026 from [crossboundary.com](https://crossboundary.com/). This records the site's visual approach as a reference for Math Is Art. It is not a specification to copy CrossBoundary's identity, images, or layout verbatim.

## Evidence and confidence

The homepage was inspected in a desktop browser and scraped for page structure and branding. Colors and font family below come from the site's extracted brand data; the visual descriptions come from direct inspection. Responsive behavior and precise spacing were not audited. Treat all Math Is Art recommendations as proposed choices.

## Observed visual system

| Element | Observed on CrossBoundary | Application to Math Is Art |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | Full-width burgundy/red field, very large white mission statement at left, compact stack of business links at right | Lead with one provocative statement and three live entry points to exhibitions |
| Typography | Aeonik (with Helvetica/sans-serif fallback); large editorial headings; sparse supporting copy | Use a strong display sans and quieter reading type, with math notation styled consistently |
| Navigation | Small identity at upper left and pill-shaped menu at upper right; header adapts as the page scrolls | Keep subjects always reachable while preserving the immersive canvas |
| Rhythm | Dense hero followed by spacious white chapter, then dark navy featured-work section | Alternate focused reading sections and high-contrast interactive exhibits |
| People chapter | Large circular portrait and oversized text with generous white space | Give mathematicians and the act of discovery human presence through stories and archival material |
| Highlighted work | Dark section with a thesis, dotted world map, colored markers, large photographic cards and previous/next controls | Use an interactive map of ideas, with linked examples and concept paths |
| Proof of impact | Numbers, virtues, project examples, editorial news | Show a learner's concrete mathematical move, a resulting conjecture, and a proof or source |

### Extracted brand tokens (source identity, not proposed site tokens)

- Typeface: Aeonik; fallback seen in browser: Helvetica, sans-serif.
- Primary blue: `#005D9F`; secondary navy: `#0C2569`; accent teal: `#169B83`.
- Base background: white; primary text: `#0A0A0A`.
- Primary button: rounded navy pill.
- The hero appears burgundy/red on screen. Its exact gradient values were not verified.

## Transferable principles

1. **A thesis before a menu.** The first screen states why the organization exists. Math Is Art should state what the visitor can *do* with mathematics.
2. **Editorial chapters with a clear tempo.** Each scroll section has one idea and one visual focus. An exhibition can follow a similar rhythm from question to experiment to theorem.
3. **A portfolio as evidence.** CrossBoundary backs its mission with specific projects. Math Is Art should back its thesis with playable mathematical examples.
4. **A visual index.** CrossBoundary's map gives work a navigable spatial form. A map of concepts can show paths among elliptic curves, symmetry, topology, and analysis.
5. **Restraint around spectacle.** Motion should expose a mathematical dependency or a user decision. It should have a purpose and a reduced-motion alternative.

## Distinctive direction for Math Is Art

Use original mathematical drawings, interactive canvases, exact notation, and a restrained atelier/notebook palette. Source illustrations from the mathematics itself. Avoid replicating CrossBoundary's photographs, logo, palette, or page composition. The visual signature should be a visitor-controlled transformation whose mathematical invariant remains visible.
